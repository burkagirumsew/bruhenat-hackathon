About The Hackathon

Pioneering Survivor-Centred Digital Transformation

Named after the Amharic expression for “Bright Mother” or “Guiding Light”, BRUH-Enat (ብሩህ እናት) provides a dedicated platform for women to architect enduring digital protection ecosystems across Ethiopia.

Organised as part of the global 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence campaign (November 25 to December 10), the initiative translates advocacy into functional, trauma-informed technology.

By uniting localized artificial intelligence, trauma-informed methodologies, and multi-sectoral institutional backing, the hackathon equips women innovators to transition bold ideas into sustainable, community-facing protection platforms.

Survivor-Centred Innovation

Prioritizing psychological safety, agency, absolute confidentiality, and zero-footprint digital architecture in every line of code.

Technology & AI-Driven Solutions

Deploying localized Natural Language Processing in Amharic, emergency disguise mechanisms, and privacy-preserving referral infrastructure.

Elevating Women in Tech

Providing an exclusive, empowering platform for women engineers, data scientists, and creators to lead high-stakes innovation.

Ecosystem Collaboration

Bridging global governance (UN Women), tech excellence (ALX Ethiopia), financial empowerment (Enat Bank), and national policy (MiNT).

Real-World Social Impact

Transitioning beyond theoretical demos into funded incubation, real-world pilot tests, and community crisis centre deployments.

Innovation Tracks

Four Pillars of Actionable Impact

Choose a challenge area that matches your passion. Each track addresses a critical structural gap in safety, protection, and community recovery.

TRACK 01

Safe Reporting & Digital Protection

Core Challenge

Make reporting abuse safer, confidential, and accessible while eliminating digital footprints that could endanger the survivor.

  • Secure digital reporting channels with zero local browser history or disguised quick-exit triggers.
  • Encrypted incident documentation tools (time-stamped notes, voice recordings) stored in secure vaults.
  • Step-by-step reporting guidance explaining legal rights and evidence preservation in plain language.
  • Privacy and safety features including dummy apps, PIN disguises, and emergency SMS beacons.

Deliverable Expectation: A functional web, mobile, or USSD prototype demonstrating end-to-end data encryption, immediate camouflage triggers, and zero data leakage if an unauthorized person inspects the device.

TRACK 02

AI-Powered Information & Support

Core Challenge

Improve access to trusted, empathetic, and culturally fluent guidance regarding health, legal, and crisis support in local languages.

  • AI-powered information assistants trained on verified, trauma-informed crisis protocols.
  • Amharic & local-language platforms supporting Afaan Oromoo, Tigrinya, and regional dialects.
  • Voice and text-based tools built for low-literacy users and low-bandwidth connectivity.
  • Smart triage guidance directing survivors to urgent clinical, emergency, and shelter services.

Deliverable Expectation: A localized conversational interface or voice agent incorporating rigorous guardrails against hallucinations, incorrect medical advice, or re-traumatizing responses.

TRACK 03

Access to Support & Service Coordination

Core Challenge

Help women find trusted support services and streamline collaboration across healthcare, shelters, legal aid, and police units.

  • Verified service directories listing accredited one-stop crisis centres and legal defenders.
  • Location-based service discovery enabling safe, offline-capable route guidance to nearby help.
  • Digital referral systems connecting caseworkers, psychologists, and clinics without exposing identities.
  • Inter-agency coordination tools to prevent survivors having to recount trauma repeatedly.

Deliverable Expectation: A secure, low-latency directory or referral workflow demonstrating provider verification, privacy-preserving case tracking, and resilient offline functionality.

TRACK 04

Prevention, Awareness & Online Safety

Core Challenge

Prevent violence before it escalates and actively combat technology-facilitated abuse, cyberbullying, doxxing, and online stalking.

  • GBV awareness platforms delivering interactive education on consent, boundaries, and rights.
  • Cyberstalking and harassment prevention toolkits for social media and smartphone hygiene.
  • Digital safety education modules tailored for secondary school and university students.
  • Warning-sign awareness tools helping individuals identify coercive control and subtle threats early.

Deliverable Expectation: An educational or intervention tool demonstrating effective behavior-change communication, threat-detection mechanisms, or digital self-defense capabilities for women.

Judging Criteria

How Solutions Will Be Evaluated

A comprehensive 100-point rubric assessing ethical safeguards, real Ethiopian impact, technical ingenuity, and local feasibility.

30% Highest Weight

Safety, Privacy & Do No Harm

How rigorously does the solution protect the survivor? Judges assess zero-trace mechanisms, end-to-end data encryption, emergency camouflage modes, consent frameworks, and risk mitigation against perpetrator retaliations.

25% Key Criterion

Impact & Relevance

Does the solution directly address real Ethiopian GBV statistics and survivor realities? Does it solve an urgent need in reporting, psycho-social care, or legal coordination with tangible community outcomes?

25% Key Criterion

Feasibility & Inclusion

Can this prototype realistically scale across Ethiopian infrastructure? Evaluates local language accessibility (Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, Tigrinya), low-bandwidth or offline readiness, USSD capability, and user simplicity.

20% Key Criterion

Innovation & Technical Execution

Originality and quality of the technical implementation. How intelligently are AI, modern web/mobile technologies, or cryptographic vaults utilized? Working quality of the prototype demonstrated on stage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers & Clarifications

Everything you need to know regarding participation, eligibility, teams, schedule, and safety standards.

BRUH-Enat ('Bright Mother' / ብሩህ እናት) is a landmark two-day hackathon hosted in Addis Ababa. It brings together women across technology, AI, design, research, and social advocacy to create practical digital tools addressing gender-based violence (GBV) in Ethiopia. It is co-organized by ALX Ethiopia, UN Women, Enat Bank, and the Ministry of Innovation and Technology (MiNT).

The hackathon is exclusively for women. We welcome software engineers, AI researchers, designers, university students, gender advocates, legal professionals, and business strategists. Multi-disciplinary perspectives are critical to creating solutions that are safe, ethical, and practical.

No, advanced technical experience is NOT required! Impactful survivor-centred solutions require problem framers, user experience researchers, domain experts in gender equality, and project pitch leaders just as much as coders. All registered participants also receive 5 evening Zoom training sessions prior to the event.

You can apply through either pathway! If you have an existing team of 3–5 women, register under 'Existing Team Application' with a designated team leader. If you apply as an individual, you will be invited to our online matching session on December 3, 2026, where we will help pair you with peers based on complementary skills.

Each team must consist of 3 to 5 women. To ensure high solution quality, we strongly encourage teams to combine both technical skills (developers, AI/data specialists) and non-technical expertise (designers, social advocates, business strategists).

There are four core innovation tracks: 1) Safe Reporting & Digital Protection, 2) AI-Powered Information & Support, 3) Access to Support & Service Coordination, and 4) Prevention, Awareness & Online Safety. Each track targets a distinct challenge area in the fight against gender-based violence.

Teams will build functional digital prototypes such as web applications, mobile tools, USSD/SMS services, localized AI conversational interfaces, or referral dashboards. Strict Rule: All demonstrations must use fictional or anonymized synthetic data. Under no circumstances should real survivor details or sensitive case files be collected or shared.

Submissions are evaluated on a 100-point rubric across four key criteria: Safety, Privacy & Do No Harm (30%), Impact & Relevance (25%), Feasibility & Inclusion (25%), and Innovation & Technical Execution (20%). The highest weighting is placed on survivor protection and safety.

The allocated prize pool includes 250,000 ETB for 1st Place, 150,000 ETB for 2nd Place, and 100,000 ETB for 3rd Place (status pending final partner ratification). Beyond cash rewards, winners and top finalists gain access to incubation pathways through MiNT Ventures, initial financing opportunities through Enat Bank, and executive mentorship.

Day 1 (Innovation Sprint) takes place in person on Saturday, December 5, 2026 at the Capstone ALX Tech Hub in Lideta, Addis Ababa. Day 2 (Final Pitch & Awards) takes place on Saturday, December 12, 2026 at the ALX Ledeta Tech Hub. Prior to the sprint, 5 online Zoom prep sessions occur nightly from November 30 to December 4, 2026 (7:00 PM – 8:00 PM EAT).

The top finalist teams receive post-event incubation guidance through MiNT Ventures and initial financing pathways through Enat Bank to help refine prototypes into viable, field-tested tools.
